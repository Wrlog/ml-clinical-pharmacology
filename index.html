Machine learning in clinical pharmacology

Can machine learning find diagnosis and predict antidepressant response?

Three R analyses on synthetic cohorts: clustering gene-expression profiles by depression diagnosis, then predicting SSRI remission from clinical, symptom and biological data with four classifiers. Figures rendered with matplotlib and seaborn; the PCA scatter plots are interactive — hover a point, zoom, or click the legend.

Key findings

  1. Gene expression separates the diagnoses cleanly. Four-cluster k-means, all hierarchical linkages and the BIC-selected GMM reproduce the four diagnostic groups exactly.
  2. Clinical factors beat chance. Using sociodemographic factors and MDD severity alone, the linear SVM reaches test AUC 0.90 and accuracy 0.82 (no-information rate 0.50).
  3. Biomarkers improve ranking, not always classification. Test AUC rises to 0.95–1.00, but at the default 0.5 threshold the RBF SVM calls every patient a non-remitter, and gradient boosting finds only about a quarter of remitters.
  4. Probabilities don't transfer across sites. Biomarker levels in the external test cohort (analysis 3) run about 30% lower than in training, so predicted probabilities shift downwards even though the ordering holds.

What's here

  1. Unsupervised clustering. 30 subjects × 10 genes. PCA, k-means, hierarchical clustering and Gaussian mixture models, scored on how well clusters recover the clinical diagnosis (adjusted Rand index).
  2. SSRI remission prediction. 400 patients with major depressive disorder (random 75/25 split). Random forest, linear/RBF SVM and gradient boosting, trained on clinical factors alone and then with 9 biological markers added.
  3. Adding symptom items. Same question with 12 baseline QIDS-SR symptom items, a predefined 300-patient training cohort and a 100-patient external test cohort.

How models were evaluated

  • Hyperparameters tuned with 5× repeated, stratified 10-fold cross-validation on the training set only.
  • Every model scored once on the held-out test set.
  • Remission (response = 1) is the positive class for sensitivity, specificity and PPV/NPV.
  • Test accuracy compared with the no-information rate (majority-class share) using a one-sided exact binomial test.

Data are synthetic teaching datasets and are not redistributed here. Only derived results are published.
